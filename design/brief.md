# gabrieldalmoro.com — Redesign Brief (v1, for approval)

## 1. What this site is

A personal home for Gabriel Dalmoro: a builder, explorer and learner.
It leads with work, but reads like the field journal of a real person.

- **For (now):** future clients, collaborators, possible employers, network.
- **For (later):** adventure audience, brand partners, sponsored content.
- **Not for:** chefs. The chef SaaS gets its own site.
- **Three words a visitor should leave with:** creative, adventurous, multipotentialite.
- **Languages:** English + French at launch.

## 2. The big idea: "Field Notes"

Gabriel already lives this way. For 260 days across South America he kept a
journal, mostly by hand: each entry starts with a date and a place, there are
lessons learned after each stretch, habit systems, budgets, grammar notes,
and a little wavy `—— // ——` mark between entries.

The site is that journal, made public and made beautiful:

- **The map is the world.** A clean cartographic base (from Direction A).
  His real route is drawn on it: Brazil → Canada (13 years) → Colombia →
  Ecuador → Peru → Bolivia → Chile → Brazil → Annecy, France.
- **His handwriting is the voice.** Notes, labels and asides use Gabriel's
  real handwriting, not a stock script font. Nobody else's site can have that.
- **The little Gabriel travels the map.** As you scroll, the avatar moves
  along the route from waypoint to waypoint and does what he did there
  (from Direction C, minus the game-island look).
- **Entries, not sections.** Each part of the page opens like a journal
  entry: `Sexta, 21 de março 2025 · Florianópolis, Brasil`.

Why it works: it holds everything together (code, surf, peaks, languages,
faith in learning) without turning into a scrapbook, and it shows the same
trait that makes him good at automation. He builds systems for his own life,
then for businesses.

## 3. Page structure

### Home (one long scroll)
1. **Opening entry.** Name, one line on who he is, the map with the avatar
   at the desk in Annecy. Handwritten aside.
2. **Now.** What he's building right now: the automation studio, the chef
   SaaS (teaser), life in Annecy. Short, dated, honest.
3. **Work.** Three to four case studies, shown large, written as entries:
   - Elisa Batch Cooking: private chef site + automations (live)
   - Sabrina Carlier: portfolio for a sommelière, broadcaster and trainer (live)
   - Lantern Clinic: first automation client (details TBC)
   - Chef SaaS: "in the works" card, links out when ready
4. **The route.** Scroll-driven map. Avatar moves through the waypoints.
   Each waypoint has a real photo and a one-line note.
5. **Languages.** PT and EN native, ES and FR professional, DE beginner,
   IT next. Shown with real handwritten notes, like his Spanish grammar pages.
6. **Say hello.** One contact point with a "why are you here?" picker:
   work together / collaborate / hiring / brand partnership.

### Other pages
- **Work with me:** the consulting offer (audit, €2,497 pilot, €7,997+
  custom, the 5-hour guarantee). Same design, focused on conversion.
- **Case study pages:** one per project.
- **Field notes (later):** short public journal entries. Optional.

## 4. Visual direction

- **Base:** light, paper-like. Topographic contour lines, a neatline frame,
  map grid references. Refined, not rustic.
- **Colour:** taken from his own photos: alpine blue, snow white,
  Atlantic-forest green, turquoise sea, golden-hour light. One accent at a time.
- **Type:**
  - Display: an elegant serif with a beautiful italic (Direction A's feel).
  - Handwriting: Gabriel's own, turned into a font or used as drawn SVG.
  - Body: a clean, warm sans.
  - Data: a monospace for coordinates, elevations, dates.
- **Signature mark:** his `—— // ——` divider, redrawn from the journal.
- **Avatar style (recommendation):** ink-and-paper. A small 3D figure with
  soft shading and a pen outline, so it looks drawn into the journal and
  sits on the map. Not glossy, not cartoon-game.
- **Photos:** real, large, with captions in handwriting and mono metadata.
